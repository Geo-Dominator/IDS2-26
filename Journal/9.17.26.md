## Journal
It's kinda insane now many things could easily ruin a design. Something that was brought up that stuck with me were the two gulfs: Gulf of Execution and the Gulf of Evaluation. These gulfs are the gap between a user's intentions and what the device allows them to do, and the difficulty of assessing how well the action worked. Something that a lot of people fail to keep in mind is when the device just... breaks. And the user needs to know when the device is broken or not. If there's an error, the user needs to know this error. If the design is just bad, the user will usually believe two things: that the device is already broken, or they did something wrong. Norman lists out 9 stages of Human decisions:
1. Goal (form the goal) 
2. Plan (the action) 
3. Specify (an action sequence) 
4. Perform (the action sequence),
5. Perceive (the state of the world)
6. Interpret (the perception)
7. Compare (the outcome with the goal)
and if any of those fail, then the design fails.
