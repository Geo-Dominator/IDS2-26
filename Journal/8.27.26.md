I should preface this by saying another class I'm taking is also using this book, so there might be times where I mention things in future chapters on accident.

My first impressions of The Design of Everyday Things, and by proxy Don Norman, is that he's EXTREMELY passionate about this stuff, for better and for worse. If you’re so knowledgeable about something that something gets named after you, you know this guy’s gonna ramble. I especially found the conversation about the three types of design shown on page 22 on the pdf, and the EXTENSIVE list of appliances that failed terribly. The battle between functionality and artistic vision, the push and pull that ignores user experience. I especially felt bad for Norman’s friend who got trapped in the European Post Office, and the couple who had degrees, but were too scared to try all the complicated settings on their washer and dryer. Makes me look at my Iphone, with all of it’s buttons and apps I never touched, or were too scared to touch, lest I break something. I also find the battle between how designers want you to use something, and how we actually use something, to be quite interesting as well. I think the Sink on page 33 and the Stairs below on page 35 sum that up perfectly. If something’s inconvenient, or accidentally conventional for another purpose, people will use that. I mean, humans love to take the path of least resistance, and if it’s easier to never use features like a sink plug, or complex settings on a washing machine, we won’t. And if it’s easier to slap on a gross sticker saying PULL to make life easier for everyone, regardless of how pretty the door looked without it, then we will.

##Favorite Quote From the Chapter

“People need some way of understanding the product or service they wish to
use, some sign of what it is for, what is happening, and what the alternative
actions are. People search for clues, for any sign that might help them cope and
understand. It is the sign that is important, anything that might signify
meaningful information. Designers need to provide these clues.” -Page 30
